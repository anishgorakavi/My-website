λOPEN SOURCE/01

GNU Prolog for Java

Keeping a logic programming system running, one improvement at a time. Maintaining and hardening an open-source Prolog implementation for the JVM.

JavaPrologMaintenance
Project notes +

I maintain GNU Prolog for Java, working on reliability and hardening. Open source gives other developers a way to inspect, extend, and contribute to the systems they use.

⌘RESEARCH / CONCEPT/02

Internet infrastructure security

What does an attacker see? Connecting DNS, IP, ASN, and routing context into an outside-in view of infrastructure risk.

DNS / BGPRPKISecurity posture
Project notes +

This research explores DNS exposure, prefix and ASN inventory, RPKI / ROA validation, route changes, infrastructure drift, and blast-radius modeling. The aim is to understand where declared security intent diverges from routing reality.

✳RESEARCH / CONCEPT/03

AI security posture

Making organizational AI use easier to understand. From service inventory and policy visibility to guided remediation.

AI governanceObservabilityAISPM
Project notes +

My work on AI Security Posture Management explores sanctioned and unsanctioned AI usage, vendor governance, accounts, policies, and traffic. The guiding model is visibility → observability → guided remediation.

≈PROTOTYPE / 2023/04

PhishLake

A continually changing threat, a continually updated dataset. Aggregating phishing intelligence from multiple reputation systems.

Threat intelligenceData pipelines
Project notes +

In 2023, I built PhishLake to aggregate phishing data from PhishTank, Google Safe Browsing, and VirusTotal. It maintained an automatically updated dataset and exposed the constant detection and evasion cycle behind phishing infrastructure.

◇CONCEPT/05

AI EduShield

Exploring a browser-based safety layer for K–12 AI use, with specialized agents and policy-controlled orchestration.

Agentic AIStudent safetyPolicy
Project notes +

AI EduShield is a concept for agents that evaluate sensitive information exposure, academic-integrity concerns, harmful content, and student safety signals. It explores how policy-controlled orchestration could support managed educational environments.

⌁PROOF OF CONCEPT/06

ID.JS / IDexe

Questioning the browser’s trust boundaries. Experiments in device-bound identity and session security.

IdentitySession security
Project notes +

ID.JS explored a relatively stable device-derived identifier. IDexe explored moving sensitive session state outside the browser and validating it through a localhost agent. These are proof-of-concept explorations, not production-ready authentication systems.

UT
The University of Texas at AustinComputer Science + Neuroscience · Class of 2030

I’m Anish, a CS + Neuroscience student at UT Austin. I’ve been programming since childhood and working in cybersecurity since my early teens.

I’m drawn to the places where fields overlap: AI and security, infrastructure and identity, computation and cognition. I like taking complicated systems apart, understanding their assumptions, and building something that makes them easier to reason about.

I also experiment with home-lab infrastructure and think about how technical ideas become useful products.

~/philosophy

Build to understand. Question assumptions.
Keep room for what comes next.

UT AUSTIN / CLASS OF 2030Academic Goals
& Successes ⌄Coursework, community, and what comes next.

As a computer science student at The University of Texas at Austin, I want to take advantage of UT’s strong CS community to explore systems, cybersecurity, networking, and the intersection of computing and neuroscience. My coursework in areas such as data structures and algorithms has strengthened the way I approach complex problems, while UT’s labs, student resources, and technical community have given me opportunities to experiment beyond the classroom.

My goal at UT is not just to earn a degree, but to leave with a deeper understanding of how computers work, from algorithms and software to operating systems and hardware. I hope to use that foundation to contribute to research, ambitious technical projects, and eventually work that connects my interests across computing, security, and neuroscience.
